Paris, 1858. The opera has started, and Paul Morphy is not watching it. He is playing chess in a private box, against two noblemen who are consulting on every move. Black answers e4 with d6, and three moves later gives up the bishop for a knight. That small trade matters. Morphy recaptures with the queen, and suddenly his pieces come out with tempo. Bishop to c4. Queen to b3, hitting f7 and b7 at the same time. Black is defending, never attacking. Then the knight takes on b5. A full piece, offered just to open lines toward the black king. Morphy castles queenside, the rook lands on the open d-file, and the pressure becomes unbearable. Rook takes d7. Rook to d1. On move sixteen, the queen goes to b8, check. Black has to take it. Rook to d8. Checkmate, with only a bishop and a rook left on the board.
