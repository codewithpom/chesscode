# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Casual-to-club chess fans who know the rules and want to understand and enjoy famous games. English and Hindi speakers, watching on YouTube from a laptop or phone. Hindi viewers expect the conversational register Indian chess creators actually use, with chess terms (fork, pin, blunder) kept in English. Videos must still make sense three months after release: evergreen games, never live tournament news.

## Product Purpose

ChessCode is a YouTube channel whose videos are produced by an automated pipeline. Python picks a game, runs Stockfish, writes commentary and voice, and emits one spec file per video; Remotion draws it. Long-form Studio videos (16:9) and Shorts (9:16) explain a game's turning points clearly while a pawn mascot supplies the comedy. Success is a channel that publishes on schedule without the owner online, with every video looking like a distinct, deliberately designed piece of work rather than a mass-produced template.

## Positioning

Serious chess explained cleanly, with comedy that lives only in one original mascot, and every video visibly built around its own game's key moment.

## Brand Personality

Calm, sharp, wry. The board, typography and narration stay serious and clean at all times; the mascot (Pyaada, a confident, dramatic pawn who is wrong about his own importance) reacts, never explains. Jokes are about the move or position, never the player.

## Anti-references

- Gradient backgrounds, glows, neon, glassmorphism, lens flares, purple-to-blue anything.
- Emoji or stock icons in the video UI.
- Meme images, stock reaction GIFs, zoom-punches or effects on every move.
- Spinning, glitch or whoosh-on-everything transitions.
- Decoration that exists only to fill space.
- Borrowed mascots or lookalikes (Claude, Duolingo's Duo).

## Design Principles

1. Serious board, funny mascot: all comedy is confined to the mascot, sound effects and timing.
2. Effects only on beats: nothing on screen moves without a reason (a move, a beat, a scene change).
3. Each video is its own: cold open, chapters, thumbnail and board theme follow that game, so no two videos look stamped from one mold.
4. Empty space stays empty: if a region has nothing to say, it stays quiet.
5. One system across languages: English and Hindi videos must feel like the same brand.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Text contrast at least 4.5:1 against its background; nothing smaller than 28 px on 1080p; tabular figures for every changing number; captions always on; the mascot never covers the board, eval bar or captions; move quality is never signalled by color alone.
